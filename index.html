About

I am an Assistant Professor of Marketing at Baylor University's Hankamer School of Business. Before joining Baylor in 2022, I was a postdoctoral scholar at Columbia Business School's Center for Decision Sciences, working with Eric J. Johnson, and taught at the University of Bath (UK). I received my PhD from the University of Colorado Boulder in 2018. I research consumer judgment and decision making, which overlaps with marketing, psychology, behavioral economics, and related fields. I have also partnered with companies, including three 10- or 11-figure firms, to plan, conduct, analyze, and/or apply the results of A/B tests or other experiments.

I study consumer decision making, often combining lab experiments with field experiments or A/B tests used in online retail. My work has been published in outlets including the Journal of Consumer Research, Journal of Marketing, PNAS, Psychological Science, JPSP, JEP: General, Journal of Consumer Psychology, Cognition, and Harvard Business Review.

        Expertise & inquiries

        My research on choice architecture and dark patterns bears directly on consumer protection: subscription sign-up and cancellation design (including ROSCA and auto-renewal practices), defaults and pre-checked boxes, hidden options, and other deceptive or manipulative online design. I also advise organizations on designing, running, and analyzing A/B tests and field experiments.

        I am open to inquiries about expert witness and testimony work, consulting, speaking, and research collaboration: Kellen_Mrkva@baylor.edu. Also, don't hesitate to reach out about collaborating on research or anything else.
